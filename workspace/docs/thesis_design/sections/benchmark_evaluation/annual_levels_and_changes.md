## 4.6 Annual rate levels and changes over time

This thesis uses borrower-specific rates both to assess official-loan concessionality in a given year and to describe changes in borrowing conditions over time. To evaluate these uses separately, the analysis compares annual rates and their year-to-year changes with primary issuance for consecutive years within 2018–2024. Each comparison requires the primary rate, the alternative rate and the DAC reference in both years. All four alternatives have smaller average errors than DAC in annual rate levels on these observations, but only IDS also has a smaller average error in annual changes.

**Table. Approximation of annual rates and their changes, 2018–2024**

| Alternative rate | Annual transitions (countries) | Level MAE: alternative | Level MAE: DAC | Change MAE: alternative | Change MAE: DAC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| IDS bondholder terms | 70 (22) | 0.657 | 1.774 | 0.609 | 0.945 |
| Secondary yields | 66 (21) | 1.185 | 1.733 | 1.463 | 0.957 |
| Rating-implied rates | 69 (22) | 1.101 | 1.769 | 1.106 | 0.973 |
| Peer estimates | 75 (23) | 1.157 | 1.743 | 1.142 | 0.988 |

*Notes:* MAE is in interest-rate percentage points relative to primary issuance. Both calculations use the same consecutive-year pairs within each row; samples differ between rows. Level MAE averages errors at both endpoints, so a year belonging to two transitions enters twice. Change MAE measures the error in the change between those endpoints. *Source:* Calculations from the constructed dataset using the error definitions in Section 4.1.

In 19 of the 75 peer comparisons, the peer rate is closer to primary issuance than the DAC rate in both years, yet its change is less accurate. The DAC reference is unchanged in all 19 cases, but that zero change is closer to the observed primary-rate movement. In 12 of these cases, the peer estimate switches from below the primary rate to above it, or vice versa, so its movement partly reflects a change in the direction of its error. The closer approximation of annual borrowing costs therefore coexists with a less accurate description of their change in these observations.

For analysis over time, this thesis consequently examines changes among the same countries and separately by source, alongside movements in the selected reference. These comparisons help identify the contribution of changing coverage and source availability, although they do not guarantee accurate changes. Each row above already holds the method fixed, and the bonds, observation dates or peer groups represented can still differ between years. The evidence also spans only six annual transitions, limiting conclusions about performance across other borrowing environments.

A separate check withholds primary issuance from selection and applies the IDS–secondary–rating-implied–peer order to the same issuer-year evidence. Across 75 transitions, the selected alternatives have level MAE of 0.688 points against 1.743 for DAC, and change MAE of 0.670 against 0.988. This check mostly selects IDS and does not select peers, so it provides little evidence about the hierarchy’s performance for non-issuers dependent on estimated rates.
