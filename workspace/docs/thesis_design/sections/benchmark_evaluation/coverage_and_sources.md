## 4.2 Coverage and the sources of available rates

The dataset supplies a selected borrowing-rate reference for 1,701 of the 1,756 low- and middle-income country-years in the sample, giving overall coverage of 96.9%. The sample contains 142 economies over 2012–2024. Each economy enters the sample in the years when it belongs to a low- or middle-income group under the retrospective classification described in Chapter 3, so the number of countries represented varies across years.

Primary issuance alone provides a reference for 215 country-years, or 12.2% of the sample. Adding IDS bondholder terms and secondary yields extends coverage to 516 country-years, or 29.4%. Rating-implied rates supply another 454 observations, bringing coverage to 55.2%, and peer estimates add 731. Estimates therefore account for a substantial part of the dataset’s reach beyond countries and years with usable issuance, IDS or secondary-market information.

Because several sources can provide a rate for the same country-year, the eligible counts overlap and cannot be added. The additional contributions count each country-year once under the selection order. Unselected rates remain available for comparisons between sources.

The share of country-years using a peer reference falls from 55.1% in 2012 to 33.1% in 2023 and 34.4% in 2024. Between 2012 and 2024, coverage without peers rises from 39.1% to 56.3%. Between 2021 and 2022, the number with a primary issuance rate, IDS terms or a secondary yield falls from 50 to 37, but the number with any selected rate falls only from 132 to 129. Rating-implied and peer estimates thus maintain broad coverage despite the reduction in observations from those three sources.

Low-income countries depend much more heavily on estimates than the two middle-income groups. Primary issuance, IDS or secondary yields cover only 22 of 389 low-income country-years (5.7%), compared with 32.0% of lower-middle-income observations and 39.9% of upper-middle-income observations. Adding rating-implied rates raises coverage without peers to 19.8%, 56.8% and 73.1%, respectively. Peer estimates supply the selected reference in 71.0% of all low-income country-years, against 41.7% in the lower-middle-income group and 25.7% in the upper-middle-income group.

Regional coverage likewise differs in the information supporting the selected rates. In sub-Saharan Africa, primary, IDS or secondary evidence covers 16.0% of country-years, and peer estimates are selected in 57.1% of all country-years. In Latin America and the Caribbean, the corresponding shares are 49.5% and 19.1%. Comparisons of borrowing conditions across these regions therefore draw to different degrees on observed borrowing information and estimates.

The dataset retains 55 country-years without a selected rate: 47 lack a reference meeting the source requirements, and eight are excluded because distress, restructuring or related circumstances prevent an ordinary borrowing-cost interpretation. An absent reference consequently cannot be read as evidence that a country was unable to borrow.

Accuracy comparisons use the smaller sample with observed primary rates, including only five low-income country-years before further matching requirements. Section 4.5 examines how far that evidence supports using estimated rates for countries without observed issuance.
