## 5.3 Borrower characteristics and differences in borrowing costs

Lower-middle-income countries have higher average borrowing rates than upper-middle-income countries in every year from 2012 to 2024 in the comparison excluding peer-only references. This thesis groups countries by their World Bank income classification in each year and retains equal-country weights. In 2024, mean rates are 11.25% for low-income countries, 9.33% for lower-middle-income countries and 7.73% for upper-middle-income countries, with respective samples of seven, 29 and 35 countries. Rates also differ within these groups. The middle half of lower-middle-income rates spans 8.24–10.75%, overlapping the upper-middle-income interval of 6.04–8.62%. Broad income categories therefore leave considerable variation in the borrowing rates against which official financing is assessed.

Six of the seven low-income rates in 2024 are rating-implied estimates, and the remaining rate is a secondary-market yield. Their mean consequently describes a small group represented mainly by estimated borrowing costs and cannot establish the average rate available to low-income countries generally. The difference between the two middle-income groups also appears in observed bond issuance: primary yields average 8.64% across nine lower-middle-income countries and 6.58% across fifteen upper-middle-income countries in 2024. The higher lower-middle-income average is thus present in actual borrowing as well as in the combined series, although these comparisons do not separate income from differences in creditworthiness, region or bond terms.

Gbohoui, Ouedraogo and Some (2023, 20) examine regulatory quality among the institutional characteristics associated with sovereign bond spreads. This thesis examines a related association using observed US-dollar issuance yields and the World Bank's Regulatory Quality indicator, which measures perceptions of governments' ability to formulate and implement sound policies and regulations (World Bank 2026). Across 215 country-years from 47 countries in 2012–2024, a one-unit higher indicator value is associated with a 1.46-percentage-point lower issuance yield after accounting for calendar-year and income-group differences.[^ch5_regulatory_quality] Better perceived regulatory quality is therefore associated with lower borrowing costs beyond the differences captured by broad income groups. This association does not establish how much a regulatory reform would reduce borrowing costs.

[^ch5_regulatory_quality]: This thesis estimates an ordinary least-squares regression with calendar-year and income-group indicators, giving each country-year equal weight. It is a simplified descriptive specification motivated by Gbohoui and coauthors. The World Bank governance estimate uses an approximately −2.5 to +2.5 scale, with higher values indicating better perceived regulatory quality. The World Bank revised its historical regulatory-quality estimates in 2025. This analysis uses those revised values, which may differ from the estimates published when the bonds were issued.
