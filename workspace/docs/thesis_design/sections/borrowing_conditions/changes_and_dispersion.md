## 5.1 Changes in borrowing costs and their spread across countries

The borrowing-rate series for 2012–2024 shows a pronounced increase in 2022, accompanied by wider differences between lower- and higher-cost borrowers. This thesis calculates the annual average across low- and middle-income countries with an eligible primary, IDS, secondary or rating-implied reference, following the selection order in Chapter 3 and excluding countries supported only by peer estimates. Giving each country equal weight is a simplifying choice, with population or economic size providing alternative bases for weighting.

The mean rises from 5.31% in 2012 to 7.37% in 2018, with some intervening declines, before falling to 5.26% in 2020. It then increases in 2021 and sharply in 2022. Country coverage expands from 54 in 2012 to 85 in 2021 and falls to 71 in 2024, so comparisons retaining the same countries are used below to examine the main movements.

Among the 81 countries represented in both 2021 and 2022, the mean rate increases by 3.44 percentage points, with increases in 77 countries. The rise also remains when the selected source is unchanged, and when primary, IDS, secondary and rating-implied rates are examined separately on their respective matched countries.[^ch5_unchanged_source] Its timing coincides with US monetary tightening from 2022 (OECD 2025, 117). The OECD describes the accompanying rise in US yields, dollar appreciation and tighter financing conditions for emerging and developing economies (OECD 2025, 117–119).

Among those same 81 countries, the gap between the 25th and 75th percentiles widens from 3.55 percentage points in 2021 to 5.03 points in 2022. This widening also appears in each of the four separate rate sources on its own matched sample. The increase in average borrowing costs is therefore accompanied by greater differences between relatively lower- and higher-cost borrowers.

The annual mean is 9.47% in 2023 before falling to 8.73% in 2024. Among the same 70 countries, however, the 2024 decline is only 0.17 percentage points, indicating that changing country coverage contributes substantially to the larger fall in the annual average.

[^ch5_unchanged_source]: Among the 49 countries retaining the same selected source in 2021 and 2022, the mean increase is 3.78 percentage points.
