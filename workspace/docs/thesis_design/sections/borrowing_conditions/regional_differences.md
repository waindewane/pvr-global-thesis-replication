## 5.2 Regional differences and their evolution

The overall increase in borrowing rates is shared across regions, but their levels differ substantially. Sub-Saharan Africa and Latin America and the Caribbean have higher average rates than East Asia and the Pacific throughout 2014–2024. By 2024, the mean is 9.97% among the 23 represented sub-Saharan African countries, compared with 7.66% among the 11 East Asian and Pacific countries. The regional trajectories also show the uneven coverage behind these comparisons: the sub-Saharan African mean represents only six countries in 2012.

OECD (2025, 121) likewise reports a rise in sub-Saharan Africa's median secondary-market yield from around 6% in 2021 to nearly 10% in 2024. Its issuance comparisons weight bonds by issuance amounts, and its secondary comparisons use medians across countries; this thesis uses equal-country means and also includes IDS terms and rating-implied estimates.[^ch5_oecd_coverage]

The 2021–2022 increase remains positive in every region when the same countries are compared. Its magnitude varies, with the smallest regional samples providing evidence for only four or five countries.

Using rating-implied rates in both years also produces a positive increase in every region. The broader comparison of 2020–2021 with 2022–2024 reaches the same conclusion when restricted to countries with a rate in every year: regional mean rates are higher in the later period across all six regions. Excluding East Asia and the Pacific raises the overall average modestly but leaves the sharp 2022 increase intact.

The combination of higher rates across regions and widening differences between borrowers shows why a single average provides an incomplete basis for assessing official lending terms. The financing conditions against which those terms are compared change both over time and across countries, whereas the DAC discount rate remains fixed within each recipient category. For an unchanged repayment schedule, the higher borrower-specific rates recorded after 2021 reduce the present value of repayments and increase the measured concession relative to market financing. Official credit consequently compares more favourably precisely when alternative borrowing becomes more expensive.

[^ch5_oecd_coverage]: The OECD secondary-yield figure carries 2023 values forward for 28 countries lacking a 2024 observation (OECD 2025, 121). Its reported 2024 regional medians therefore include earlier observations.
