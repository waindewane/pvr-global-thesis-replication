## IDS bondholder borrowing terms

The World Bank’s IDS Bondholders data and the primary series cover overlapping bond borrowing, but measure its terms differently. The primary series combines yields on individual eligible issues; IDS reports the average contractual interest rate on new public and publicly guaranteed external debt commitments to bondholders in each country and year. The World Bank weights these interest rates by the amounts committed (World Bank, n.d.-a). Unlike an issue yield, a contractual interest rate does not capture the effect of issuing above or below face value. Using IDS bondholder rates as a borrower-specific reference builds on ONE’s *Priced Out* methodology, which combines them with secondary-market yields and rating-based estimates (ONE Data n.d., “The discount rate”).

IDS can provide broader coverage because it includes borrowing by other public bodies and private borrowers with public guarantees (World Bank, n.d.-a). The country-year Bondholders aggregate also combines commitments whose currency composition is not identified in the series used here. IDS is therefore retained as a distinct borrowing-rate proxy, with broader borrower coverage and without the USD-only restriction applied to the primary bond benchmark. Its agreement with primary issuance is evaluated with this overlap in underlying borrowing in mind.

The analysis uses positive IDS rates and excludes observations whose interpretation as a borrowing rate remains unresolved.[^ids-inconsistent-terms] A figure that may represent only a floating-rate margin is withheld because the margin excludes the reference interest rate needed to calculate the full interest charge.
