## Selecting a borrowing-rate reference

The dataset retains primary issuance rates, IDS bondholder terms, secondary-market yields, rating-implied rates and peer approximations separately. When an application requires one borrowing-rate reference for a country-year, this thesis selects the first eligible rate in that order. Eligibility depends on the source-specific construction requirements and the suitability of the observation for the borrowing-cost comparison. Each selected rate retains its source, timing and currency information.

Primary issue yields receive priority because they describe the terms on which the sovereign raised financing. IDS bondholder terms come next because they also concern new borrowing during the year. The two series cover overlapping bond borrowing, although IDS reports contractual interest rates rather than issue yields. Its public and publicly guaranteed debt coverage can include borrowers beyond the central government, and the country-year series used here does not identify the currencies of the underlying commitments (World Bank, n.d.-a). These differences affect comparability without making IDS a separate, independent observation of the same borrowing conditions.

After primary issuance rates and IDS terms, the selection uses secondary yields, which describe the market pricing of outstanding bonds around year-end and can supply observations in years without new issuance. If none of these sources provides an eligible rate, the selection proceeds to the rating-implied estimate and then to the peer approximation. This thesis chooses the sequence to prioritize evidence on new borrowing, followed by market pricing and estimates. The benchmark-evaluation chapter assesses how closely the different sources approximate observed issuance rates and compares the main selection with an alternative that places secondary yields before IDS. The prescribed order does not itself establish an accuracy ranking.

For a floating-rate loan, the interest charged consists of a reference interest rate plus an additional margin. A reported figure that may contain only that margin cannot establish the total borrowing cost and is withheld if its interpretation remains unresolved. Distress and restructuring can likewise affect whether a bond yield represents the cost of new borrowing on ordinary contractual terms. Exclusion depends on the evidence and intended use, rather than a high rate alone. When the country's circumstances prevent using an ordinary borrowing-cost estimate, the procedure does not replace an excluded observation automatically with a rating-implied or peer rate.

If no source supplies an eligible reference, the country-year remains in the dataset without a selected rate. Missing source information, the absence of observed issuance and evidence of financing difficulties remain distinguishable. The absence of an observed bond issue does not by itself establish that a country was unable to borrow.

The benchmark evaluation and concessionality applications also use selections that exclude peer approximations or restrict the evidence to primary issuance and IDS. The main official-loan applications exclude peer-only references and report the broader selection separately. Because these restrictions can change both the rate and the sample, differences between the resulting estimates are interpreted with reference to the loans and countries included in each comparison.

When a country's selected source changes between years from an annual new-commitment rate to a year-end secondary yield, the measured movement may reflect the different measurement dates as well as changing borrowing conditions. Even within the same year, secondary yields observed in December can differ from yields on bonds issued earlier because market conditions changed in the intervening months. The analysis of borrowing conditions across countries and over time therefore examines individual sources and comparisons that hold the method fixed alongside the selected series.
